Welcome to your free study book

This original study guide gives junior secondary students a starting point for school-based scholarship tests, academic competitions and general junior-level entrance practice. It is not an official publication of any examination body. Always check the current syllabus and rules for the particular competition you plan to enter.

How to use it: Read one chapter at a time, write notes in your own words, attempt the practice questions without looking at the answers, then check the explanations. Revisit topics you find difficult.

Chapter 1: How to prepare for competitive exams

Understand the examination

Find out the subjects tested, question format, time allowed, number of questions, permitted materials and marking rules. Use information from the organiser or school rather than rumours.

Make a realistic study plan

Study in short, focused sessions. Review a concept, solve questions and later correct mistakes. Include breaks and enough sleep.

Practise actively

Close the book and explain a concept from memory. Solve fresh problems, show your working and review every incorrect answer. Understanding a method is more useful than memorising an answer.

Manage examination time

Read instructions carefully. Answer questions you understand first when the rules allow it. Keep track of time and reserve a little time to review your work.

Learn from mistakes

Keep a correction notebook with the topic, correct method and lesson learned. Revisit it during revision.

Chapter 2: English Language

Parts of speech

A noun names a person, place, thing or idea. A pronoun replaces a noun. A verb expresses an action or state. An adjective describes a noun. An adverb modifies a verb, adjective or another adverb. A preposition shows a relationship, often of place or time. A conjunction joins words or clauses.

Example: In “The careful student answered quickly,” careful is an adjective and quickly is an adverb.

Subject–verb agreement

A verb should agree with its subject in number and person. A singular third-person subject normally takes a present-tense verb ending in -s or -es: “She reads.” A plural subject takes the base form: “They read.”

Tenses

Present tense describes habits or facts: “She studies every day.” Past tense describes completed events: “We visited the library.” Future forms describe later events: “They will arrive tomorrow.”

Comprehension and summary

Read for the main idea, then identify supporting details. Answer from the passage when asked what it says. A summary keeps central points and removes repetition and unnecessary examples.

Vocabulary and spelling

Use context to work out unfamiliar words. Synonyms have similar meanings; antonyms have opposite meanings. Check how a word functions in its sentence.

Chapter 3: Mathematics

Order of operations

Use brackets first, then indices, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right. Example: 8 + 3 × 4 = 8 + 12 = 20.

Fractions

A fraction represents part of a whole. For addition or subtraction, first use a common denominator. Example: 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4. To make an equivalent fraction, multiply or divide numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.

Percentages

Percent means out of 100. To find 20% of 150, calculate 20/100 × 150 = 30. To convert a fraction to a percentage, multiply it by 100%.

Ratio and proportion

A ratio compares quantities. The ratio 2:3 has five parts. If 25 items are shared in the ratio 2:3, one part is 25 ÷ 5 = 5; the shares are 10 and 15.

Algebra

Letters can represent unknown numbers. Solve x + 7 = 15 by subtracting 7 from both sides: x = 8. Apply the same operation to both sides of an equation.

Geometry and measurement

Perimeter is the distance around a shape. Area measures the surface inside a 2D shape. Rectangle perimeter = 2(length + width); rectangle area = length × width. Use units such as cm and cm².

Data handling

Mean = sum of values ÷ number of values. Median is the middle value after arranging data in order. Mode is the most frequent value. Range = highest value − lowest value.

Chapter 4: Verbal Reasoning

Analogies

An analogy tests a relationship between pairs of words. Example: Bird is to nest as bee is to hive. Identify the relationship first (animal and home), then find a pair with the same relationship.

Classification

Choose the item that does not belong. Example: mango, orange, pawpaw, carrot. Carrot is different because it is commonly classified as a root vegetable, while the others are fruits.

Word relationships

Practise synonyms, antonyms, categories, word completion and alphabetical order. Check the exact meaning of each word.

Logical statements

Use only the information given. If all roses are flowers, every rose is a flower. It does not follow that every flower is a rose.

Chapter 5: Quantitative Reasoning

Number patterns

Look for a repeated operation or changing difference. In 3, 6, 9, 12, each term increases by 3, so the next term is 15. Check several steps before deciding the rule.

Sequences and operations

Try addition, subtraction, multiplication, division or alternating rules. In 2, 4, 8, 16, each term is multiplied by 2, so the next term is 32.

Word problems

Identify what is known and what is asked, choose the correct operation, calculate and check whether the answer makes sense. Keep units consistent.

Tables and charts

Read the title, labels, scale and units before interpreting a chart. Compare the correct rows or columns and do not confuse totals with individual values.

Chapter 6: Basic Science

Living and non-living things

Living things carry out life processes such as nutrition, respiration, growth, excretion, reproduction, sensitivity and movement. Non-living things do not carry out all these processes as living organisms do.

Cells and organisation

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. In many organisms, cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs work together in systems.

Matter

Matter has mass and occupies space. Common states are solid, liquid and gas. Heating and cooling can change a substance from one state to another.

Force and energy

A force is a push or pull that can change an object's motion or shape. Energy is the capacity to do work or cause change. Forms include heat, light, sound, electrical and chemical energy.

Environment and health

Keep surroundings clean, use safe water, dispose of waste properly and follow good hygiene. Plants, animals, air, water and soil interact in ecosystems.

Chapter 7: General Knowledge and Civic Awareness

Citizenship

A citizen is a legally recognised member of a country. Civic responsibilities include obeying lawful rules, respecting others, protecting public property and participating peacefully in community life.

Government

In Nigeria, the three arms of government are the Legislature, Executive and Judiciary. Their roles include making laws, implementing laws and interpreting laws respectively.

Geography

Learn the states and capitals of Nigeria, major rivers, neighbouring countries, map directions and physical features. Use a current atlas or reliable school reference to practise locations.

Current affairs

Current-affairs facts can change. Study recent, dated information from reliable news sources and official government or competition-organiser publications. Always check the date.
